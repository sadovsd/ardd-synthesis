These are a list of questions that my mentor Arjun Krishnan gave to use when studying new literature.

1. List 3 things you learned that you didn’t know before. These can be about background, methods, or anything else. 


2. What are some specific ways in which this paper makes you think differently and/or give you ideas about your own project(s)? 


3. What topics did this paper make you want to read/learn more about? 


4. What are the goals of the study? What is the motivation for the work (i.e., why should this work be done; what’s the benefit)? Also, list 2-3 key ideas/findings from past work that this work is based on. 


5. In your own words, what is the take home message of this paper? i.e., what do you hope to remember about this work? 


6. List the few things you didn’t understand about the paper (so we can discuss them). 


7. What’s the most important or interesting thing(s) you saw in the supplemental data? 


8. Are the data presented in a manner that allow you to interpret them? Is any relevant information missing? Is each figure/table clear and/or quantified (what’s on the axes/rows/columns; what’s being plotted/tabulated)? 


9. If you look at the data (figures and tables) alone, do you reach the same conclusions as the authors do? Do the data suggest an alternative explanation? 


10. Suggest at least two orthogonal (new) analysis not in the paper that might strengthen the results or might have been included instead.
