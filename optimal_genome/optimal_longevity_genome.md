
## Genomic Stability

1. SIRT6 centenarian haplotype
rs201141490 — p.Asn308Lys, N308K
rs183444295 — p.Ala313Ser, A313S
The two substitutions occur together in cis and are called centSIRT6.

Compared with ordinary SIRT6, centSIRT6 showed:
Increased mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase activity
Better stimulation of double-strand-break repair
Stronger suppression of LINE1 retrotransposons
Stronger interaction with and ribosylation of lamin A/C
More effective killing of cancer cells
Surprisingly, weaker deacetylase activity

2. FOXO3 regulatory longevity alleles
rs2802292-G
rs12206094-T
rs4946935-A

rs12206094-T and rs4946935-A were associated with longevity in European cohorts.
Both longevity alleles increased reporter activity.
Both were associated with higher FOXO3 expression in several tissues.
rs4946935-A altered SRF transcription-factor binding.
Their regulatory effects changed in response to IGF-1 and cellular conditions

3. RAD50/IL13 locus
rs2706372-T

RAD50 is part of the MRE11–RAD50–NBN complex, which detects DNA double-strand breaks, holds broken DNA ends together, and coordinates ATM signaling and repair.

4. OGG1
rs1052133 — p.Ser326Cys
OGG1 removes oxidized guanine lesions, especially 8-oxoguanine, through base-excision repair. The review itself notes that experimental OGG1 activation can increase oxidative-DNA-damage repair.

## Epigentic alterations

SIRT6 N308K/A313S
Strong cellular function; weak human association

SIRT6 rs350845 and linked eQTLs
Human association plus expression effect

However, the rare missense haplotype itself was only nominally enriched among centenarians and did not reach statistical significance in the original cohort. A separate intronic allele, rs350845, was significantly associated with reaching 100 and acts as a SIRT6-expression eQTL in numerous tissues.

KANSL1 - mild GOF or expression-increasing regulatory variants
Higher genetically predicted expression associates with human longevity. Participates in the NSL histone-acetylation complex. Causal allele remains unresolved because the locus is structurally complex.

KAT7
Look for partial LOF or reduced-expression variants. KAT7 suppression reduced cellular senescence and extended lifespan in mice. Complete LOF could impair stem cells and development, so partial or tissue-specific reduction is the plausible direction.

SIRT7
Look for mild GOF or expression-preserving variants. Loss causes chromatin relaxation, genomic instability, metabolic dysfunction, and premature aging in mice. No established human longevity variant.

SETDB1
Look for targeted/mild GOF variants that improve repeat repression. Maintains H3K9me3 and represses LINE-1 elements. Worth searching for variants that strengthen repeat-specific recruitment without globally silencing genes.

TRIM28
Look for mild GOF or recruitment-enhancing variants
Works with SETDB1 to maintain heterochromatin and repress retrotransposons. No established human longevity variant; excessive activity could silence normal genes.

CBX5 (HP1α)
Look for mild GOF. Preserves heterochromatin architecture. Increased HP1 activity extends lifespan in invertebrates, but mammalian longevity evidence is limited.

PIN1.
function-preserving or mild GOF variants. Supports heterochromatin and genome stability; loss is associated experimentally with premature aging and neurodegeneration.

DNMT1
Look for fidelity-enhancing variants
Maintains methylation patterns during replication. The ideal variant would reduce age-related methylation drift without indiscriminate hypermethylation. No known longevity allele.

UHRF1
Look for fidelity/recruitment-enhancing variants/ Recruits DNMT1 to newly replicated DNA. Potential target for improving methylation maintenance, but both over- and underactivity can promote disease.

TET2
Look for function-preserving/ TET2 loss drives age-related clonal hematopoiesis and inflammation. Variants protecting TET2 activity may support longevity

SUV39H1
mild or repeat-targeted GOF variants. Generates protective H3K9me3 heterochromatin. Could limit age-related repeat activation, but excessive activity may impair plasticity and DNA repair.
